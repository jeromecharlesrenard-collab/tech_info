Manufacturing Processes

Additive vs. Subtractive

TopicsAM · Subtractive · CNC · Formative
Additive Manufacturing (AM)
Builds the part layer by layer from a 3D model (3D printing). Little waste, no tooling, complex shapes (lattices, internal channels); slower per part and usually weaker between layers.
Subtractive Manufacturing
Removes material from a solid block: milling, turning, drilling, EDM. Tight tolerances and full material strength, but more waste and limits on internal shapes.
CNC
Computer Numerical Control: the machine follows a G-code program. Used for both: CNC mills and lathes (subtractive) and 3D printers (additive) are all numerically controlled. See Machinery & Equipment.
Formative
The third family: molding and casting (injection molding, vacuum casting) shape material in a mold; cheapest per part at volume, but the mold costs time and money.
Rule of thumb
1 – 10 parts: 3D print or CNC; 10 – 50: vacuum casting; thousands: injection molding.

3D Printing Processes

ProcessesFDM · SLA · SLS · MJF
FDM
Fused Deposition Modeling (also FFF): a nozzle melts plastic filament and lays it down line by line. Cheapest and most common (desktop printers); PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA, TPU, nylon, PC. Visible layer lines; weakest across layers (Z direction).
SLA
Stereolithography: a UV laser (or screen, in MSLA/DLP printers) cures liquid photopolymer resin. Finest detail and smoothest surface (miniatures, dental, jewelry molds); parts need washing (IPA) and UV post-curing; standard resins are brittle.
SLS
Selective Laser Sintering: a laser fuses nylon powder (PA12, PA11). The loose powder supports the part, so no support structures; strong functional parts with a slightly grainy surface.
MJF
Multi Jet Fusion (HP): inkjet heads print a fusing agent on nylon powder, then infrared heat fuses each layer. Like SLS but faster, with more even strength; parts come out gray, usually dyed black.

Vacuum Casting

TopicsMaster · Silicone mold · PU resin · Short runs
How it works
A master part (often an SLA print) is cast into a silicone mold; liquid polyurethane (PU) resin is then poured into the mold under vacuum, so there are no air bubbles.
Run size
About 15 – 25 parts per mold; ideal for 10 – 50 parts.
Strengths
Production-like finish and colors; resins that mimic ABS, PP, PC, or rubber (wide Shore hardness range); no costly steel mold.
Limits
Molds wear out; slower per part than injection molding; parts are PU, not the real thermoplastic.

MoldSilicone, made from a printed master
MaterialPU resin poured under vacuum (no bubbles)

Plastics at a Glance

Plastics15 types · Commodity · Engineering · High performance
Thermoplastics
Melt and re-melt: can be printed, molded, welded, and recycled. Every plastic below except some PU forms is a thermoplastic.
Thermosets
Cure once and never melt again (epoxy, most PU foams and casting resins, SLA resins).
Classes
Commodity (cheap, huge volume: PE, PP, PVC, PS, PET) → engineering (stronger, tougher: ABS, PC, PA, POM) → high performance (heat and chemical proof, expensive: PEEK, PEI, PTFE).
