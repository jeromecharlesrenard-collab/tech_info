Milling Machine

ActionSpinning cutter
MaterialsMetals, plastics
Best forFlat faces, slots

A spinning multi-tooth cutter removes metal while the part, clamped on an X-Y table, moves under it; the spindle or knee sets the Z depth.

Types
Vertical (knee mill, the classic "Bridgeport" style) or horizontal spindle; manual or CNC.
Tools
End mills, face mills, drills, reamers, taps; a digital readout (DRO) shows the table position.

Lathe

ActionSpinning part
MaterialsMetals, plastics
Best forRound parts

The part turns in a chuck while a single-point tool moves along or across it, so everything it makes is round.

Operations
Turning (outside diameter), facing (end), boring (inside), threading, drilling from the tailstock, knurling, parting off.

CNC Mill & Lathe

ActionComputer control
MaterialsMetals, plastics
Best forRepeat parts

CNC (Computer Numerical Control) mills and lathes follow a G-code program made in CAM software, so every part comes out the same.

Axes
3-axis mill (X, Y, Z) for most parts; 4- and 5-axis machines add rotation to reach several faces in one setup.
Extras
Automatic tool changer, coolant, probing; a CNC lathe is often called a turning center.

Turn-Mill Center

ActionTurn + mill
MaterialsMetals
Best forOne-setup parts

A CNC lathe with live (driven) tools, often a Y axis and a sub-spindle, so it turns, mills flats, and drills cross holes without moving the part to another machine.

Drill Press

ActionVertical drilling
MaterialsMost materials
Best forSquare holes

Drills holes square to the table with steady pressure; also used for reaming, countersinking, and tapping. Clamp the part; never hold it by hand.

EDM

ActionElectric sparks
MaterialsConductive only
Best forHardened steel

Removes metal with thousands of small electric sparks in a dielectric fluid; there is no cutting force, so it works on any conductive metal, however hard.

Wire EDM
A thin brass wire cuts through profiles (punches, dies, gears) with very tight tolerances.
Sinker (ram) EDM
A shaped electrode "burns" its mirror image into the part: mold cavities, blind pockets.
Hole-drill EDM
Fast small holes, e.g. the start hole for a wire EDM cut.
Limits
Slow; conductive materials only.

CNC Router

ActionGantry spindle
MaterialsWood, plastics
Best forLarge panels

A large flat bed (often 4 × 8 ft) for cutting and carving panels; faster but less rigid than a CNC mill, so it is meant for softer materials.

Cutting

ItemRemember
LaserFinest cut on thin to medium sheet
PlasmaFast and cheap on thick steel; metal only
Water jetCuts almost anything, no heat; slowest
Band sawVertical = curves; horizontal = bar to length
ShearStraight sheet cuts, no kerf, no heat
Cut-off wheelQuick abrasive chop cuts (sparks and burrs)

Laser Cutter

ActionLight beam
MaterialsSheet, acrylic
Best forFine detail

Very narrow kerf and clean edges on sheet metal, acrylic, and wood; an assist gas (oxygen, nitrogen, or air) blows the melt away.

Types
Fiber lasers for metals (steel, stainless, aluminum); CO₂ lasers for wood, acrylic, and other non-metals.
Caution
Never cut PVC or vinyl: it releases toxic, corrosive chlorine gas.

Plasma Cutter

ActionIonized-gas arc
MaterialsConductive metals
Best forThick steel

Cuts any electrically conductive metal quickly, by hand or on a CNC table; the edge is rougher, with a wider heat-affected zone, than laser or water jet.

Water Jet Cutter

ActionWater + abrasive
MaterialsAlmost anything
Best forNo-heat cuts

Water at ~60,000 psi or more, mixed with garnet abrasive, cuts metal, stone, glass, composites, and rubber, with no heat-affected zone or warping.

Pure water jet
Without abrasive, for soft materials: foam, rubber, gaskets, food.
Limits
Slower and costlier to run than plasma; slight taper on thick cuts.

Band Saw

ActionBlade loop
MaterialsMetal, wood
Best forCurves

The vertical band saw cuts shapes by hand-feeding the part across its table; blade speed is set for the material (slow for steel, fast for wood and aluminum).

Horizontal Bandsaw

ActionSelf-feeding blade
MaterialsBar, tube, pipe
Best forCut to length

The stock is held in a vise; the saw head feeds down by itself and shuts off at the end of the cut. Many models also swivel for miter cuts.

Sheet Metal Shear

ActionBlade on blade
MaterialsSheet metal
Best forStraight cuts

Cuts sheet like scissors, with no kerf, no heat, and no sparks; foot-operated for thin sheet, hydraulic (guillotine) for thicker plate.

Disk Wheel Cutter

ActionAbrasive disk
MaterialsBar, tube, angle
Best forQuick chop cuts

The abrasive chop saw grinds through metal fast but leaves heat, sparks, and a burr; a cold saw (slow toothed blade) gives a cleaner, burr-free cut.

Brake Press

ActionPunch into V-die
MaterialsSheet, plate
Best forStraight bends

Also called a press brake: bends sheet and plate along a straight line. A CNC back gauge sets each bend position; capacity is rated in tons and bed length.

Punch Press

ActionPunch and die
MaterialsSheet metal
Best forHoles, blanks

Punches holes and shapes in one stroke; a CNC turret punch holds many tools and fires hundreds of hits per minute for high-volume sheet parts.

Hydraulic Press

ActionHydraulic force
MaterialsMetal parts
Best forPress, straighten

Slow, controlled force rated in tons (a 20-ton H-frame shop press is common): pressing bearings and bushings in or out, straightening shafts, forming, and stamping.

Pipe Bender

ActionBend die
MaterialsPipe, tube
Best forKink-free bends

Bends pipe and tube without kinking or flattening; a mandrel inside the tube supports tight bends. A bend is specified by its centerline radius (CLR).

Joining

ItemRemember
MIGWire-fed: fastest and easiest (the "glue gun")
TIGCleanest and most precise; aluminum and stainless
SpotClamp + current: overlapping sheets, no filler
Laser welderNarrow weld, little heat, little warping
RivetsNo heat; join different metals (pop rivets from one side)

TIG / MIG / Spot Welder

ActionArc or current
MaterialsMost metals
Best forPermanent joints

Three common welding methods: MIG for speed, TIG for precision and looks, spot welding for overlapping sheet.

MIG (GMAW)
Wire electrode fed through the gun with shielding gas (often 75% argon / 25% CO₂ for steel); fast, easy to learn, the workhorse of steel fabrication.
TIG (GTAW)
Non-consumable tungsten electrode, filler rod added by hand, pure argon; slow but the cleanest, most precise welds; best for thin metal, aluminum, and stainless.
Spot (resistance)
Two copper electrodes clamp overlapping sheets and pass a high current that melts a small "nugget"; no filler or gas; car bodies and sheet metal.
Stick (SMAW)
Also common: flux-coated rod, no gas bottle; good outdoors and on dirty or rusty steel.

Laser Welder

ActionLaser beam
MaterialsThin sheet
Best forClean seams

Narrow, deep welds with a small heat-affected zone, so there is little warping or cleanup; handheld fiber laser welders are now common in fab shops.

Riveter

ActionCold joint
MaterialsSheet metal
Best forMixed metals

A cold joint (no heat, no warping) that also joins metals that cannot be welded together; blind (pop) rivets install from one side.

Solid rivets
Set with a pneumatic rivet gun and a bucking bar on the far side; the standard on aircraft.

Grinding & Finishing

Angle grinderHand tool that cuts, grinds, and cleans (change the disk)
Belt / disc sanderBelt = flat faces; disc = edges and angles
Sand blasterStrips rust and paint; preps for coating

Surface Grinder

ActionAbrasive wheel
MaterialsHardened steel
Best forFlat precision

The part sits on a magnetic chuck while a grinding wheel takes very light passes; the go-to machine for flat, parallel, hardened steel parts.

Angle Grinder

ActionHandheld disk
MaterialsMetals
Best forCut, grind, clean

The most versatile hand tool in the shop: the disk sets the job. Always keep the guard on and wear a face shield.

Disks
Cut-off wheel (cutting), grinding wheel (weld removal), flap disk (blending, finishing), wire wheel (rust and paint), surface-conditioning pads.

Belt/Disc Sander

ActionBelt and disk
MaterialsMetal, wood
Best forDeburr, shape

The belt flattens faces and edges; the disk (with a tilting table and miter gauge) finishes angles and convex curves. Coarse grit removes, fine grit smooths.

Sand Blaster

ActionAir + abrasive
MaterialsMetals
Best forStrip rust, paint

Cleans and textures surfaces before paint or powder coat, usually in a blast cabinet. Silica sand is a health hazard (silicosis), so shops now use other media.

Media
Aluminum oxide (aggressive), glass bead (satin finish), walnut shell or plastic (gentle), steel grit.
See also
Tumbling, for finishing many small parts at once, and all part finishes: Fabrication.

Part Washer

ActionSpray, soak, brush
MaterialsMetal parts
Best forOil, chips, grime

Cleans oil, coolant, chips, and grime off parts before inspection, welding, painting, or assembly.

Types
Solvent sink washer (brush and recirculating solvent); aqueous spray cabinet (hot water and detergent); ultrasonic tank for small, precise parts and blind holes.

Inspection & Scanning

ItemRemember
Surface plateThe flat zero for layout and measuring (never a workbench)
3D scannerPart → point cloud → mesh → CAD
Reverse engineeringMaking a CAD model from a real part

Layout Table (Surface Plate)

ActionFlat reference
MaterialsGranite or iron
Best forLayout, inspection

A precision-flat granite (or cast-iron) plate used as the zero reference for marking out parts and checking flatness, height, and squareness.

Used with
Height gauge and layout fluid (Dykem) for scribing lines; dial and test indicators, squares, gauge blocks, V-blocks.
Grades
AA (laboratory), A (inspection), B (toolroom).
Care
Keep it clean and covered; never hammer, weld, or stack parts on it.

Handheld 3D Laser Scanner

ActionLaser line scan
MaterialsMost parts
Best forPart to CAD

The process is called 3D scanning or reverse engineering: sweeping the scanner over the part builds a point cloud that turns into a mesh model.

Workflow
Scan (point cloud) → mesh (STL) → CAD model you can edit, with software like Geomagic Design X; also used to inspect a part against its CAD model.
Examples
Creaform HandySCAN; FARO portable measuring arms with a laser line probe.
Tips
Shiny, dark, or clear surfaces may need a thin scanning spray; many handheld scanners use small positioning-target stickers on or around the part.

Tools

TypeHand and power
Key specSAE and metric
Best forEveryday work

The everyday kit: hand tools, measuring tools, and portable power tools.

Hand tools
Wrenches, sockets, screwdrivers, pliers, hammers, files, clamps, taps and dies, deburring tools.
Measuring
Calipers, micrometers, steel rule, squares, protractor, height gauge, dial indicators, thread gauges.
Power tools
Drill, impact driver, angle grinder, reciprocating saw, die grinder.

Toolbox

TypePortable box
Key specCarry handle
Best forTools on the go

Carries a working set of tools to the machine, the job site, or the car; steel or tough plastic, with a lift-out tray.

Rolling Tool Chest

TypeDrawer cabinet
Key specLocking drawers
Best forOrganized storage

A top chest on a rolling cabinet with ball-bearing drawers; foam drawer inserts (shadow boards) show at a glance when a tool is missing.

Work Bench

TypeHeavy-duty table
Key specSteel or wood top
Best forAssembly, vise

The main work surface, with a bench vise bolted to one corner: a steel top for welding and hammering, a wood top for assembly and delicate parts.

Tool/Part Cart

TypeRolling cart
Key specLocking casters
Best forMoving parts

Brings tools and parts to the machine and moves work between stations; shelves with raised lips keep parts from sliding off.

Overhead Crane/Hoist

TypeLifting device
Key specRated capacity
Best forHeavy lifts

Lifts heavy parts, molds, and stock onto machines: bridge or gantry crane, jib crane, with a manual or electric chain hoist.

Safety
Never exceed the rated capacity; inspect slings, hooks, and chains before use; never stand or walk under a load.
