Metals

Stainless Steel

Grades304 · 316 · 17-4 PH · 410 · 430 · 440C
What it is
Steel with at least 10.5% chromium, which forms a thin, self-healing oxide layer that resists rust.
Families
  • Austenitic (300 series): non-magnetic, very corrosion resistant, not hardened by heat treatment
  • Ferritic (400 series): magnetic, no nickel, cheaper
  • Martensitic (400 series): magnetic, hardenable by heat treatment
  • Precipitation hardening (PH): heat treated with aging for very high strength
304 SS
General purpose, corrosion resistant; austenitic "18-8" (18% chromium, 8% nickel), the most common stainless: kitchen and food equipment, tanks, hardware.
316 SS
Marine and chemical grade, higher corrosion resistance; 304 plus ~2% molybdenum, which resists salt and chlorides: boat fittings, chemical and medical equipment.
17-4 PH SS
High strength, precipitation hardened; 17% chromium, 4% nickel, plus copper; heat treated to conditions such as H900 (hardest) or H1150 (toughest): aerospace parts, shafts, valves.
410 SS
Martensitic, hardenable, wear resistant; ~12% chromium, the basic hardenable stainless: valves, pump parts, cutlery, fasteners.
430 SS
Ferritic, magnetic, moderate corrosion resistance; ~17% chromium, no nickel, so it is cheaper: appliance panels, trim, dishwasher liners.
440C SS
High hardness, bearing and cutting-tool grade; high carbon (~1%), the hardest common stainless at ~58 – 60 HRC: ball bearings, knife blades, valve seats.

Carbon & Alloy Steel

Grades1018 · 1045 · 4130 · 4140 · 4340 · A36 · A514 · AR400/AR500
Grade numbers
AISI/SAE four digits: the first two give the alloy (10xx = plain carbon, 41xx = chromium-molybdenum, 43xx = nickel-chromium-molybdenum); the last two give the carbon in hundredths of a percent (1018 = 0.18% carbon). More carbon = harder and stronger, but harder to weld.
1018
Low carbon, good weldability, general purpose; easy to machine (cold rolled), can be case hardened: pins, brackets, shafts, fixtures.
1045
Medium carbon, higher strength, machinable; can be flame or induction hardened: shafts, axles, gears, bolts.
4130
Chromoly, high strength, weldable; great strength-to-weight in thin-wall tube: race-car roll cages, bicycle and aircraft frames.
4140
Chromoly, high strength, wear resistant, heat treatable; often sold pre-hardened (~28 – 32 HRC): shafts, gears, tooling, fixtures.
4340
High-strength alloy, aerospace and heavy duty; nickel adds toughness: landing gear, crankshafts, heavy axles.
A36
Structural grade, the most common fabrication steel; ASTM plate and shapes; the "36" is its minimum yield strength, 36 ksi (250 MPa): beams, angles, base plates.
A514
High-strength structural, quenched and tempered ("T-1"); yield 100 ksi (690 MPa), almost 3× A36: cranes, heavy equipment, bridges.
AR400 / AR500
Abrasion-resistant plate; ~400 / 500 Brinell hardness; hard to bend and machine: dump-truck beds, buckets, chutes, AR500 shooting targets.
Note
4130, 4140, 4340, and A514 are low-alloy steels rather than plain carbon steels, hence this box's title.

Grade codeLast 2 digits = carbon (1018 = 0.18%)
1018Mild steel: welds and machines easily
41xxChromoly: strong; 4130 welds, 4140 hardens
4340Toughest: landing gear
A36Structural workhorse (36 = 36 ksi yield)

Aluminum

Grades6061-T6 · 7075-T6 · 5052 · 2024 · 3003 · 6063 · 5083
Series
The first digit gives the main alloying element: 2xxx copper, 3xxx manganese, 5xxx magnesium, 6xxx magnesium + silicon, 7xxx zinc. 2xxx, 6xxx, and 7xxx are heat treatable; 3xxx and 5xxx are hardened by cold working.
Tempers
-T6 = solution heat treated and artificially aged (full strength); -H32 = strain hardened and stabilized (common on 5052 sheet).
6061-T6
General purpose, good strength, machinable; weldable and corrosion resistant, the default choice: frames, plates, fixtures, enclosures.
7075-T6
Highest strength, aerospace grade; nearly 2× the strength of 6061, close to many steels; poor weldability, less corrosion resistant: aircraft parts, high-stress components.
5052
Excellent corrosion resistance, formable; handles salt water and bends easily: boat hulls, fuel tanks, sheet metal enclosures.
5083
Marine grade, weld-friendly; the strongest non-heat-treatable alloy, keeps its strength after welding: ship hulls, pressure vessels, cryogenic tanks.
2024
High strength, fatigue resistant, aerospace; poor corrosion resistance (often clad with pure aluminum, "Alclad") and poor weldability: aircraft skins and structures.
6063
Extrusion grade, architectural and structural profiles; smooth surface that anodizes well, lower strength than 6061: window frames, railings, T-slot framing, trim.
3003
General purpose, good formability, low strength; cheap and easy to work: cookware, trim, ductwork, roofing.
ItemRemember
WeightAbout 1/3 of steel
6061The default aluminum: strong, weldable, machinable
7075Strongest, but do not weld it
5052 / 5083Marine: 5052 sheet bends, 5083 welds
6063Extrusions (window frames, T-slot)

Other Metals

MetalsCopper alloys · Titanium · Nickel · Refractory · Light · Heavy
Copper
Electrical, heat transfer, plumbing; the best conductor after silver: wire, bus bars, heat exchangers, pipe.
Brass
Fittings, valves, decorative; copper + zinc, machines very easily (C360 free-machining), gold look; also instruments.
Bronze
Bushings, bearings, marine; copper + tin (or aluminum, phosphorus); low friction, resists salt water: boat propellers and hardware.
Beryllium copper
High strength, electrical conductivity, tooling; the strongest copper alloy, non-sparking: springs, connectors, mold inserts, safety tools. Machining dust is toxic.
Titanium
Aerospace, medical, high strength, low weight; about 45% lighter than steel with similar strength (Grade 5, Ti-6Al-4V), very corrosion resistant, biocompatible.
Nickel
Plating, high-temperature alloys; also the nickel in 300-series stainless and in batteries.
Inconel
High temperature, aerospace, exhaust systems; nickel-chromium superalloy that keeps its strength red-hot: turbines, rocket engines, race exhaust. Hard to machine.
Cobalt alloys
Wear and high temperature, medical implants: Stellite hard-facing and valve seats; cobalt-chrome hip and knee implants.
Zinc
Die casting, galvanizing: Zamak die-cast parts; hot-dip galvanizing coats steel with zinc, which corrodes first and protects the steel.
Magnesium
Lightweight structural, aerospace; the lightest structural metal (~1.8 g/cm³, about 2/3 of aluminum): wheels, housings, laptop frames. Chips and dust are flammable.
Tungsten
Tooling, high temperature, wear resistant; the highest melting point of all metals (3,422 °C) and very dense (19.3 g/cm³): TIG electrodes, counterweights; base of tungsten carbide.
Molybdenum
High-temperature tooling, alloying element; melts at ~2,620 °C: furnace parts; the "moly" in chromoly steel and 316 stainless.
Lead
Radiation shielding, weights; dense (11.3 g/cm³) and soft; also batteries. Toxic: handle with care.

Composites & Other Materials

Fiber Composites

TypesGFRP · CFRP · Kevlar · Laminates · SMC / BMC · CMC · MMC
What it is
Fibers (strength) held in a matrix (usually resin) that shares the load; FRP = Fiber-Reinforced Polymer. How they are made: see Fabrication (composite layup).
Fibers
  • Fiberglass (GFRP): glass fiber + resin; general structural, cheapest
  • Carbon fiber (CFRP): high strength, low weight; aerospace and performance
  • Kevlar (AFRP): aramid fiber; impact and ballistic resistant
  • Basalt fiber: volcanic rock fiber; corrosion resistant, low cost
  • Boron fiber: very high stiffness; aerospace and defense
  • Natural fibers: flax, hemp, jute + resin; eco grade
Laminates
Garolite G10 / FR4: fiberglass-epoxy laminate for electrical and structural panels (FR4 = flame retardant, the circuit-board base). Phenolic laminates: paper or fabric + phenolic resin; wear resistant (bushings, insulators).
Molding compounds
SMC (Sheet Molding Compound): chopped fiber + resin sheets, pressed into automotive panels. BMC (Bulk Molding Compound): dough-like, molded into structural and electrical parts.
Thermoplastic composites
PEEK/CF, PA/CF: carbon fiber in a meltable plastic; high performance, can be reshaped, welded, and 3D printed.
Advanced matrices
CMC (Ceramic Matrix): very high temperature, aerospace and defense. MMC (Metal Matrix, e.g. aluminum + silicon carbide): wear resistant. Carbon-carbon (graphite): extreme temperature, aircraft and race brakes, heat shields.

CompositeFiber for strength + resin to hold it
FiberglassCheap, all-purpose
Carbon fiberStrongest for its weight
KevlarImpact and bullets
G10 / FR4Fiberglass board (circuit boards)
Carbon-carbonExtreme heat: brakes, heat shields

Sandwich Cores

CoresHoneycomb · Balsa · Foam · Syntactic foam
Idea
Two thin, strong skins glued to a light core: like an I-beam, the core spaces the skins apart, giving a very stiff, light panel.
Nomex honeycomb
Aramid paper core; aerospace sandwich panels (floors, interiors).
Aluminum honeycomb
Lightweight metal core; aerospace panels, energy absorbers.
Balsa core
End-grain balsa wood; lightweight sandwich for boat hulls and decks, aerospace.
Foam core
PVC or PET foam; marine and aerospace sandwich, does not rot.
Syntactic foam
Resin filled with hollow glass microspheres; light yet crush resistant: deep-sea buoyancy, aerospace.
ItemRemember
SandwichStrong skins + light core = stiff and light
HoneycombNomex or aluminum: aerospace
BoatsBalsa or foam cores
Syntactic foamGlass bubbles: deep sea

Other Materials

MaterialsWood · Rubber · Glass · Quartz · Concrete · Aerogel · Graphite
Wood / plywood
Still widely used for jigs and fixtures: cheap, easy to cut, and kind to finished parts.
Rubber / elastomers
Gaskets, seals, vibration damping: nitrile (oil), EPDM (weather), neoprene, silicone (heat), Viton (fuel, chemicals).
Glass
Tempered (4 – 5× stronger, breaks into small cubes), laminated (holds together when broken), borosilicate (thermal shock resistant, labware).
Quartz / fused silica
Very pure glass: optical, semiconductor, high temperature; almost no thermal expansion.
Concrete / UHPC
Structural and architectural fabrication; UHPC (Ultra-High Performance Concrete) is several times stronger than normal concrete, for thin precast panels.
Aerogel
Ultra-low density, mostly air: the best solid thermal insulator.
Pyrolytic graphite
Layered graphite that spreads heat very well along its plane: thermal management, aerospace.
ItemRemember
WoodCheap jigs and fixtures
RubberSeals and damping
GlassTempered = strong; laminated = holds together; borosilicate = heat proof
AerogelBest insulator (mostly air)

Ceramics

Technical Ceramics

TypesAlumina · Zirconia · SiC · Si₃N₄ · B₄C · WC · HAp · Cordierite
Traits
Very hard, heat and wear resistant, usually electrical insulators, but brittle; shaped by pressing and sintering, finished by diamond grinding.
Alumina (Al₂O₃)
General purpose, electrical insulation, wear resistant; the most common technical ceramic (spark-plug insulators, wear liners).
Zirconia (ZrO₂)
Highest strength and toughness of the ceramics: dental crowns, medical, ceramic knives.
Silicon carbide (SiC)
Extreme hardness, high temperature, abrasion resistant: abrasives, pump seals, armor, kiln parts.
Silicon nitride (Si₃N₄)
High strength, thermal shock resistant: hybrid ceramic bearing balls, engine and cutting-tool parts.
Boron carbide (B₄C)
The hardest of these (after diamond and cubic boron nitride), and light: ballistic armor, blast nozzles.
Tungsten carbide (WC)
Cutting tools, wear parts; the densest here (~15.6 g/cm³); usually bonded with cobalt ("carbide" end mills and inserts).
Hydroxyapatite (HAp)
Biomedical: the mineral of bone; implant coatings and bone grafts.
Cordierite
Thermal shock resistant: kiln shelves, catalytic-converter honeycombs.
ItemRemember
CeramicsHard and heat proof, but brittle
AluminaThe everyday ceramic: insulators
ZirconiaToughest: dental crowns
Boron carbideArmor
Tungsten carbideCutting tools ("carbide")

Resins, Epoxies & Glues

Resins

TypesPolyester · Vinylester · Epoxy · Phenolic · PU · BMI · PEEK
Resin vs. epoxy
Resin is the broad term for the matrix that holds fibers or fills a mold; epoxy is one specific resin chemistry, the highest-performance common category.
Polyester
General purpose, low cost: fiberglass laminating (boats, tubs).
Vinylester
Stronger, chemical and corrosion resistant: tanks, pipes, boat hulls.
Epoxy
Highest strength: aerospace and structural composites (see Epoxies).
Phenolic
High temperature, fire resistant: electrical laminates, ablative heat shields.
Polyurethane
Flexible to rigid: casting and encapsulation (also vacuum casting).
Bismaleimide (BMI)
High-temperature aerospace composites, above epoxy's range.
Cyanate ester
Extreme temperature, low dielectric loss: aerospace, radomes, satellites.
PEEK
Ultra-high-performance thermoplastic matrix (melts rather than cures).
ItemRemember
ResinThe broad term; epoxy is one type
PolyesterCheapest (boats, tubs)
VinylesterChemical resistant
EpoxyStrongest
Phenolic / BMI / cyanateHigh heat

TypesBPA · BPF · Novolac · Marine · Film · Conductive
Bisphenol-A (BPA)
The most common structural epoxy.
Bisphenol-F
Lower viscosity, more chemical resistant: coatings, flooring.
Novolac
High temperature, strong chemical resistance: tank linings.
Cycloaliphatic

Flexible

Conductive
Silver-filled: electronics, EMI shielding.

Glues & Adhesives

GroupsInstant · Structural · Flexible · Wood · Tapes & films · Specialty
Instant and fast
  • Cyanoacrylate (CA, super glue): instant bond, rigid, most materials
  • Contact cement: rubber and laminate; bonds instantly on contact
  • Hot melt (EVA): fast, low strength; packaging and assembly
  • UV-cure: fast, precise; optical and electronic parts
Structural
  • Epoxy (2-part): high strength, fills gaps
  • Acrylic (MMA): high strength, impact resistant; bonds metals and plastics with little prep
  • Construction adhesive: general heavy duty, fills gaps
  • MS polymer (modified silicone): flexible, structural, paintable; construction
Flexible and sealing
  • Polyurethane (PU glue): flexible, waterproof, expands as it cures
  • Silicone: flexible, high temperature, waterproof sealant
  • Neoprene: flexible, heat resistant; automotive and industrial
  • Rubber cement: flexible, repositionable; paper and gaskets
Wood and porous
  • PVA (polyvinyl acetate, wood glue): porous materials, woodworking
  • Hide glue: traditional, reversible with heat and water; instruments
  • Cementitious: tile, stone, masonry
Tapes, films, sprays
  • Pressure sensitive (PSA): tapes and labels, repositionable
  • Spray adhesive: large areas; foam, fabric, laminate
  • Thermoplastic film: heat activated; aerospace laminates
Specialty
  • Anaerobic (Loctite): cures without air, between metal parts; threadlocking, retaining, gasketing
  • Conductive (silver epoxy): electronics, EMI shielding

Strength

Strength & Material Properties

TermsTensile · Yield · Impact · Stiffness · Hardness
Tensile strength
The maximum pull (stress) a material takes before it breaks (ultimate tensile strength, UTS).
Yield strength
The stress where it starts to bend for good (permanent deformation); below it, the part springs back. Parts are designed to stay below yield.
Impact strength
Energy absorbed in a sudden hit without breaking (Charpy or Izod test) = toughness.
Stiffness
Resistance to flexing under load (elastic modulus); steel ~200 GPa, aluminum ~70 GPa (1/3), so aluminum bends 3× more for the same shape.
Hardness
Resistance to scratching and denting (Rockwell HRC, Brinell HB).
Units
1 ksi = 1,000 psi ≈ 6.9 MPa.
