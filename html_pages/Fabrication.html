Main Fabrication Technologies

Subtractive (Material Removal)

MethodsMachining · Grinding · EDM · Laser · Waterjet · Plasma
Milling
A rotating multi-point cutter removes material from a stationary or moving workpiece: flat surfaces, pockets, slots, contours, and complex 3D shapes. Runs on 3-axis through 5-axis machines.
Turning (lathing)
The workpiece rotates while a stationary single-point tool removes material: shafts, bushings, threads, and tapers. Swiss-type lathes handle small, high-precision parts.
CNC machining
Computer-controlled milling, turning, routing, drilling, and grinding, driven by G-code from CAM software; repeatable, tight-tolerance parts from prototypes to mid volumes.
Drilling / boring / reaming
Making holes, enlarging them, and finishing them to a precise diameter and surface finish.
Grinding
An abrasive wheel removes small amounts of material for very tight tolerances and fine finishes. Types: surface, cylindrical, centerless, jig.
EDM
Electrical Discharge Machining: controlled sparks erode conductive material. Wire EDM cuts profiles; sinker EDM forms cavities. Suited to hardened steels and mold tooling.
Laser cutting
A focused laser beam melts or vaporizes sheet or plate: fast, precise, narrow kerf; metals, plastics, and wood.
Waterjet cutting
A high-pressure water stream, often with abrasive, cuts almost any material with no heat-affected zone.
Plasma cutting
An ionized gas arc cuts conductive metals; faster and cheaper than laser on thick plate, but less precise.
ItemRemember
MillingThe tool spins (flat faces, pockets)
TurningThe part spins (round parts)
GrindingTightest tolerances, finest finish
EDMSparks: hardened steel, molds
CuttingLaser = precise; plasma = thick and cheap; waterjet = no heat

Formative (Shaping Without Removal)

MethodsMolding · Extrusion · Casting · Forging · Sheet metal
Injection molding
Molten thermoplastic is injected under pressure into a closed steel or aluminum mold. High tooling cost, but very low unit cost at high volume, with excellent repeatability.
Extrusion
Material is pushed through a die to make continuous constant-section profiles: tubes, rods, channels, frames. Aluminum, plastics, rubber.
Blow molding
An air-inflated parison (hollow tube of hot plastic) is shaped against a mold: hollow parts like bottles and tanks. Variants: extrusion, injection, and stretch blow molding.
Rotational molding
Plastic powder in a rotating heated mold coats the walls: large, seamless hollow parts with low tooling cost.
Thermoforming / vacuum forming
Heated plastic sheet is drawn over a mold by vacuum or pressure: trays, enclosures, packaging.
Compression / transfer molding
Material is pressed into a heated mold cavity; common for thermosets, rubber, and composites.
Casting
Molten metal is poured or injected into a mold.
  • Sand casting: cheap molds, large parts
  • Die casting: high volume, aluminum and zinc
  • Investment casting (lost wax): fine detail
  • Permanent mold casting: reusable metal molds
Forging
Metal is shaped by compressive force, hot or cold, which aligns the grain for superior strength. Open-die, closed-die, and roll forging.
Stamping / press forming
Sheet metal is cut and formed in dies (blanking, punching, bending, drawing, coining); progressive dies allow very high volume.
Deep drawing
Sheet is pulled into a die cavity: seamless cups, cans, housings.
Sheet metal bending
Press brakes bend sheet along straight lines: brackets, chassis, enclosures.
Roll forming
Strip passes through successive roller stations: long, uniform profiles.
Metal spinning
A rotating disc is formed over a mandrel: axisymmetric parts like cones and domes.
Powder metallurgy / MIM
Metal powder is pressed, or injection molded with a binder (MIM, Metal Injection Molding), then sintered: near-net-shape small, complex metal parts.
ItemRemember
Injection moldingCostly mold, pennies per part at volume
Hollow plasticBlow molding (bottles); roto molding (big tanks)
ExtrusionPush through a die: same profile all along
CastingPour metal: sand, die, investment (lost wax)
ForgingSqueeze metal: strongest parts
Sheet metalStamp, draw, bend, roll, spin

Additive (Layer by Layer)

MethodsFDM · SLA / DLP · SLS / MJF · DMLS / SLM · Binder jetting · DED
FDM / FFF
Extruded thermoplastic filament, layer by layer: low cost, prototypes and fixtures.
SLA / DLP
UV light cures liquid photopolymer resin: high resolution, smooth surfaces.
SLS / MJF
A laser (SLS) or fusing agent plus heat (MJF) fuses nylon powder: strong functional parts, no supports needed.
DMLS / SLM
Metal 3D printing: a laser fuses metal powder into dense, complex metal parts for aerospace, medical, and tooling.
Binder jetting
A binder bonds powder layers, then the part is sintered: faster and cheaper for metal or sand at volume.
DED
Directed Energy Deposition: material is melted as it is deposited; large parts and repair.

Joining & Assembly

MethodsWelding · Brazing / soldering · Ultrasonic · Adhesives · Fasteners
Welding
Metals are fused with heat: MIG, TIG, stick, spot/resistance, laser, or friction stir welding (a spinning tool stirs the metals together without melting them).
Brazing / soldering
Parts are joined with a lower-melting filler metal, without melting the base metal (brazing above ~450 °C / 840 °F, soldering below).
Ultrasonic welding
High-frequency vibration bonds plastics or thin metals; common for plastic housings.
Adhesives & fasteners
Structural adhesives, rivets, screws, snap fits, and press fits.

Composites & Other

MethodsComposite layup · Chemical etching · Surface finishing
Composite layup
Carbon or glass fiber plus resin: hand layup, prepreg cured in an autoclave, RTM (Resin Transfer Molding), filament winding, or pultrusion. High strength-to-weight parts.
Chemical etching
Also photochemical machining: selective chemical removal makes thin, precise metal parts and PCBs.
Surface finishing
Anodizing, plating, powder coating, painting, heat treatment, passivation: adds corrosion resistance, hardness, or a better look. Mechanical finishing: see Tumbling below.

CompositesFiber + resin: light and strong
EtchingChemicals cut thin parts and PCBs
FinishingAnodize, plate, powder coat, heat treat

Finishing

Tumbling (Mass Finishing)

TopicsBarrel · Vibratory · Ceramic media · Plastic media · Shapes
What it does
Parts tumble with abrasive media, water, and a compound to deburr edges, round corners, and smooth or polish surfaces, many parts at once.
Barrel tumbler
A rotating drum: the load slides and cascades. Simple and cheap; slower (hours to days); good for large batches of small parts.
Vibratory tumbler
A vibrating bowl or tub: the load rolls in a spiral. Several times faster than a barrel, gentler on parts, and the parts can be checked during the run.
Ceramic media
More aggressive on both edges and surfaces; heavy and dense, so it cuts faster = less time. Best for hard metals: steel, stainless, titanium.
Plastic media
Less aggressive on surfaces but still deburrs edges, with more time. Lighter, leaves a smoother finish; best for soft metals (aluminum, brass, zinc) and before plating or anodizing.
Other media
Steel (burnishing to a bright shine); walnut shell or corn cob (drying and polishing).
Media shapes
  • Cones: reach into corners, holes, and recesses
  • Tetrahedrons (triangles, pyramids): sharp points for edges and inside corners
  • Others: cylinders, angle-cut cylinders, spheres, stars, ovals
Choosing size
Media must be clearly larger or smaller than any hole or slot, so it never gets stuck (lodging). Smaller media = finer finish and better reach; larger = faster cutting.
Loading
About 3 parts media to 1 part parts by volume, so parts do not hit each other; keep the compound and water topped up to keep the media cutting.
